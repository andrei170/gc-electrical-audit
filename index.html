Finding 1 — Your Credentials Are Buried
For an electrician this matters more than for any other trade
"Part P" mentioned once, in body copy "18th Edition" mentioned once, in body copy NICEIC or NAPIT not found anywhere on the site Insurance not published Star rating none shown

Electrical is the one trade where a homeowner genuinely fears getting it wrong. A bad paint job is annoying. A bad consumer unit is a house fire and an invalid insurance policy. So the first thing they look for is evidence you are certified — and if they cannot see it in a few seconds, they assume you are not.

You are Part P compliant, you notify building control, and you fit 18th Edition consumer units. All of that is on your site. It is just written as a sentence in a paragraph rather than shown as a badge at the top of the page.

We could not find a NICEIC or NAPIT registration anywhere. If you hold one, publishing it is the single highest-value change on this document. If you do not, say so on the call and we will build the trust case a different way rather than claim something you cannot evidence.

Finding 2 — Nothing Is Measured. At All.
Read from your served page source
Google Analytics (GA4) ✗ not present Universal Analytics ✗ not present Google Tag Manager ✗ not present Ads conversion tracking ✗ not present Meta pixel ✗ not present

There is no measurement of any kind. No visit, no call, no form submission is recorded. So nobody can say how many people find you, whether the site produces work, or which service brings it in.

It is the cheapest thing on this page, and it has to come before any advertising — Google's bidding needs conversion data to optimise against, and with none it optimises for clicks.

Finding 3 — The Commercial Side Has No Route In
You have commercial references and no commercial page

Your testimonials include Keystone Construction Ltd and York Student Property. Those are exactly the clients a small electrical contractor wants more of — repeat work, scheduled, invoiced rather than chased.

But your navigation reads Home, Contact, Welcome, More. A site manager or a letting agent arriving on your site finds a general electrician's page and nothing addressed to him: no commercial section, no mention of certification for landlords, no EICR or periodic inspection route, no insurance figures.

Landlord certification alone is recurring, legally required work in a market like Ripon and Harrogate. It is the most predictable revenue available to a domestic electrician and your site does not mention it.

Finding 4 — The Site Is Heavy For What It Contains
Roughly 558KB on a page with four navigation items

That is not catastrophic, but it is several times what a page this size needs, which is typical of a Wix build carrying a large image set. It costs you most with the customer who matters — somebody on mobile data who has just found a problem and is comparing two electricians.

Related, and easier to fix: your navigation labels are Home, Contact, Welcome, More. “Welcome” and “More” tell a visitor nothing and tell Google less. Naming them after what you actually do — rewires, consumer units, EICRs, commercial — is a ten-minute change with a real return.

What Is Genuinely Good
More of this is right than the grade suggests
  • Your title tag is well written. “Electrical Contractor | GC Electrical Services Ripon | Kirkby Malzeard” — trade and two locations. Most trades sites we audit have nothing like this.
  • A working contact form with six fields including address and subject. Most of the sites we look at have no form at all.
  • Tap-to-call and a published email address. Both routes open.
  • Five named testimonials, two of them commercial clients. Named references beat anonymous praise every time.
  • A genuinely broad service list — consumer units, sub-mains, underfloor heating, fire alarms, LED upgrades, EV-adjacent work, emergency call-out.
  • Part P certification with building control notification stated plainly. That is the right claim, in the wrong place on the page.
